Ducati — a power tool

An industrial-design study: what would a cordless drill look like if a motorcycle maker built it? Sculpted bodywork, a carbon-weave grip and a steel chuck — emotion, stance and speed, brought to a hand tool. A concept study, not a Ducati commission.

01Overview
A tool that wants to be ridden.

Ducati doesn't make drills. This study asks what one would look like if it did — taking the marque's visual language of sculpted volumes, red-on-carbon, and a low aggressive stance, and applying it to an everyday cordless power tool.

The work runs from a wall of quick silhouettes through to a resolved hero render: bodywork in Ducati red, a carbon-weave grip, and a bare steel chuck where the speed lives.

02From Sketch to Form
03

The Idea

Borrow the emotion, not just the badge.

The brief the study sets itself — "what moves you?" — is about transferring feeling, not decals. A Ducati reads as fast standing still; the drill tries for the same: weight pulled low and back, a forward-raked body, hard creases catching light along the barrel.

04

The Form

Ten silhouettes test where the motor mass sits, how the grip meets the body, and how aggressive the nose can get before a tool stops looking usable. The resolved version keeps the drama but lands the ergonomics — a grip you'd actually hold, a chuck you'd actually trust.
